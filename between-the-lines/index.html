Worksheet

Row numbers follow the lesson's worksheets. Every increment is rounded to the nearest second before it is added, as on paper. The Next step button under the diagram fills this table one row at a time; All fills it at once.

The method

One value controls the table

A table of houses is entered by sidereal time. The same ST has one Midheaven but many Ascendants, one for each latitude.

The ST ratio

Native's ST minus the earlier ST in the table, divided by the interval between columns (4′). It says how far across the box the native lies.

Increment = difference × ratio

Subtract the earlier table value from the later one, multiply by the ratio, add the result to the earlier value. That is the whole of interpolation.

The Ascendant twice over

Interpolate along the ST at the lower latitude, then at the higher latitude, then between those two by the latitude ratio. Move from the lower-latitude value toward the higher-latitude one.

South of the equator

Add or subtract 12h to the LST, look up as normal, then keep the Midheaven's original sign and give the Ascendant the opposite of what is listed.

Planets from the ephemeris

The ratio is the hour of birth in UT divided by 24. Multiply the day's motion by it and add to the position at 00:00 — or subtract, if the planet is retrograde.

Why it differs from your program

Interpolation assumes the value grows evenly between two cells. On the sphere it does not: the great circles curve, and the Ascendant changes faster in some signs than others. That is why tables are cut at 4′ of ST — coarser steps would be wildly inaccurate — and why an interpolated result is always a little off the direct calculation. Within 1′ is fine.

The other source is rounding: a program keeps fractions of a second in the sidereal time, and multiplying them by 15 for the RAMC turns them into whole seconds of arc. The last row of each worksheet shows the direct value so you can see the gap.

Direct calculation

Midheaven: MC = arctan(tan RAMC ÷ cos ε), adding 180° if the result lands far from the RAMC. Ascendant: ASC = arctan( −cos RAMC ÷ (sin RAMC · cos ε + tan φ · sin ε) ), placed in the right quadrant. Here ε is the obliquity and φ the latitude. The table on this page is built from these with ε = 23° 26′, which reproduces the lesson's printed table to the second.
