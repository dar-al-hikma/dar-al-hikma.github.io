The rules

Carry and borrow in sixties

60″ make 1′; 60′ make 1°. When a column reaches 60, keep the remainder and carry 1 leftward. When you cannot subtract, borrow 1 from the left as 60.

The circle is 360°

Past 360°, subtract 360°. If the top of a subtraction is smaller than the bottom, add 360° to the top first.

Into decimals: divide leftwards

Seconds ÷ 60 join the minutes; minutes ÷ 60 join the degrees. Five decimal places are plenty.

Into degrees: multiply rightwards

Fraction × 60 gives minutes; the new fraction × 60 gives seconds. Round the seconds.

Absolute longitude

Each sign begins 30° after the last, counting from 0° Aries. Add the sign's start to the degrees within the sign; divide by 30 to go back.

Time and arc

The sky turns 15° in an hour. Multiply time by 15 for arc; divide arc by 15 for time.

The twelve signs

If a result is off by exactly 30°, a sign's starting degree is wrong. If it is off by 360°, the wrap was forgotten.

Why sixty

The Babylonians counted in sixties, and every astronomer since has inherited it: a circle of 360°, a degree of 60 minutes, a minute of 60 seconds. Sixty divides evenly by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20 and 30, so fractions of a circle rarely leave a remainder. The zījes of Baghdad were written in this notation, in letters of the alphabet rather than numerals, and a chart drawn by hand today still is.

You will not often add longitudes on a printed chart. But Lots are distances between two points, a table of houses is entered by sidereal time, and a longitude becomes a time correction only by dividing it by fifteen — and then the calculator comes out.
